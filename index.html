Free-trade detector

Rule 12 charges max(1%, discount), not both added together. So a trade priced 1% or more better than the close for one side pays that discount back instead of the 1% fee: that side trades for free, and the key on the other side pays the 1% fee and loses the discount. Because keys cost nothing, throwaway keys can absorb those losses on every trade. This panel watches the close1 room live and flags every trade priced 1% or more away from the latest reference price.

This is a sample: it reads up to 200 new close1 messages every 10 s from when you opened the page, and trades also happen in other registered rooms. The close each trade settles at is only known at its sweep, so the deviation is measured against the latest posted reference. Trades shown are room messages, not proof they settled; the referee decides that. The fix for close-2 is fee = 1% + max(0, discount).

How this is computed

  1. The page reads /r/<room>/export for d-close1-price, -pnl, -positions and -state, and keeps only posts signed by the referee key named in the seed. It checks every Ed25519 signature over <room>|<nonce>|<text> with WebCrypto, keeping each nonce as raw digits.
  2. In the fold, score is linear in price: value_at(s) = cash + Σ q·s for longs and −q·(2p − s) for shorts, so the slope is the net position. A key's position comes from the referee's positions list when it is there. Otherwise it is fitted by least squares over that key's last 36 published scores against the mark, and only kept when the fit is tight (max residual ≤ 1 POLF).
  3. Settlement score = published score + position × (Hyperliquid ref − global mark). The basis column is the price at which that key's score would be zero.
  4. Prize shares follow the fold's tie rule: tied keys share the places they span. The rules do not say how the pool splits between places, so the page assumes equal thirds of 1,000,000 FLOP. It only ranks the keys the referee has published, so keys outside the published 25 are unknown and can outrank them.
